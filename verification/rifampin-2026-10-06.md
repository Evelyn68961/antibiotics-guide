### A1 · Adult dose

<span color="blue">`PO`</span> on an empty stomach (1 h before or 2 h after a meal, with a full glass of water); give ≥1 h before antacids. Always combine with other active drugs (never monotherapy).<br>**TB:** 10 mg/kg PO once daily, max 600 mg/day (US). UK: 8–12 mg/kg, <50 kg 450 mg, ≥50 kg 600 mg. TW 仿單: 600 mg qd; elderly or frail 10 mg/kg qd. Usual regimen 2HRZE → 4HR (ATS/CDC/IDSA 2016).<br>**LTBI (guideline):** 4R = 10 mg/kg qd (max 600 mg) × 4 months, or 3HR = INH + RIF daily × 3 months (NTCA/CDC 2020).<br>**Meningococcal carrier:** 600 mg PO q12h × 2 days (US/UK). TW 仿單 says 600 mg qd × 4 days, an outdated regimen.<br>**Hib household prophylaxis (UK):** 20 mg/kg qd (max 600 mg) × 4 days.<br>**Serious staphylococcal infection / brucellosis / Legionnaires' (UK):** 600–1200 mg/day in 2–4 divided doses, always with another active antibiotic. Guidelines: staphylococcal PVE 300 mg q8h ≥6 weeks (AHA 2015); staphylococcal PJI with retained implant 300–450 mg PO BID (IDSA 2013).<br>**Leprosy (UK):** 600 mg once monthly, or 10 mg/kg daily, with ≥1 other anti-leprosy drug.<br>US: avoid intermittent dosing >600 mg once or twice weekly (flu syndrome, haematologic and renal reactions).

**Why:** New entry with an empty column. US label INDICATIONS AND USAGE (Tuberculosis): "Adults 10 mg/kg, in a single daily administration, not to exceed 600 mg/day, oral ... administered once daily, either 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal with a full glass of water." Meningococcal Carriers: "600 mg rifampin be administered twice daily for two days." UK SmPC 4.2: "single daily dose in tuberculosis is 8 – 12 mg/kg ... less than 50 kg – 450 mg ... 50 kg or more – 600 mg"; brucellosis, Legionnaires' or serious staph "600 – 1200 mg given in 2 - 4 divided doses, together with another appropriate antibiotic"; H. influenzae "20 mg/kg once daily (maximum daily dose 600 mg) for 4 days"; leprosy "600 mg ... once per month". TW 仿單 3.2.1/3.2.2: "口服600mg，一日一次，共4日" (carrier) and TB 600 mg qd, elderly 10 mg/kg. US PRECAUTIONS General: "Doses of rifampin greater than 600 mg given once or twice weekly have resulted in a higher incidence of adverse reactions, including the 'flu syndrome'". Regimens come from the guidelines cited. Only the PO route applies because the hospital stocks only the 300 mg capsule. The hospital site writes 'orally/IV' and must not be copied.

### A2 · Renal dose, HD, CRRT

No adjustment at ≤600 mg/day (TW 仿單 6.7, US Clin Pharm, UK 5.2). t½ is unchanged in renal failure at ≤600 mg/day. Use caution at >600 mg/day (UK 4.4): after a single 900 mg dose, t½ rose from 3.6 h to 5.0 / 7.3 / 11.0 h at GFR 30–50 / <30 / anuric (US).<br>**HD / PD:** not removed (TW 5.1.9); no supplement; usual dose (ATS/CDC/IDSA 2016: no change).<br>**CRRT:** no label data; usual dose expected because elimination is mainly biliary [flag: no CRRT study cited].<br>AKI on rifampin is usually hypersensitivity interstitial nephritis or ATN, mostly with intermittent or resumed therapy, not accumulation (US AR Renal; TW 6.7).

**Why:** Empty column. The TW insert is for the stocked product and is the first choice for renal dosing. TW 6.7: "腎功能不全的病人，不需要降低劑量". TW 5.1.9: "本藥不會由血液透析或腹膜透析而自體中移除". ### A3 · Hepatic dose

**Contraindicated in jaundice** (UK 4.3; TW 4 禁忌).<br>Hepatic impairment: use only if necessary, under close supervision. ### A9 · Mechanism

Rifamycin. Binds the β-subunit of bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase and blocks RNA transcription; does not inhibit the mammalian enzyme. Bactericidal, including against slow or intermittently growing M. tuberculosis, and acts both intra- and extracellularly. Resistance arises by single-step RNA-polymerase mutation, so never use as monotherapy; cross-resistance with other rifamycins.

**Why:** US Microbiology: "inhibits DNA-dependent RNA polymerase activity ... does not inhibit the mammalian enzyme ... resistance ... single-step mutations ... likely to be resistant to other rifamycins". TW 10.1: "與細菌的DNA-依賴性RNA聚合酶的β次單元之強力結合". UK 5.1: bactericidal "intracellularly" and against rapidly growing extracellular organisms.

**Sources:** US FDA label CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY – Microbiology – https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=50f706f9-5003-4e15-bece-85b6213b1f5c; Taiwan 仿單 §10.1 – https://mcp.fda.gov.tw/im_detail_1/%E8%A1%9B%E7%BD%B2%E8%97%A5%E8%A3%BD%E5%AD%97%E7%AC%AC044061%E8%99%9F; UK SmPC 5.1 – https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6382/smpc

### A11 · Pregnancy

US: no adequate controlled human studies. Crosses the placenta, but decades of published data have not linked rifampin with major birth defects or miscarriage. Animals: spina bifida (rats) and cleft palate (mice) at about 0.2–2× MRHD. Use if benefit outweighs risk. Standard first-line TB drug in pregnancy (ATS/CDC/IDSA 2016).<br>Last weeks of pregnancy: postnatal haemorrhage in mother and infant, so give vitamin K (US; UK 4.6).<br>FDA letter categories are retired; the TW 仿單 still prints "Category C" and this should not be cited as current.

**Why:** Empty column. US Pregnancy–Teratogenic Effects: "published data over decades of use have not established an association with rifampin during pregnancy and major birth defects or miscarriage ... spina bifida ... Cleft palate". Non-Teratogenic: "can cause postnatal hemorrhages in the mother and infant for which treatment with vitamin K may be indicated." UK 4.6 is the same (vitamin K1). TW 6.1 prints "FDA Pregnancy Category(懷孕用藥級數)：C"; per the ground rules this must not be written as current.
