### A9 · Mechanism

Nicotinamide analogue prodrug. Mycobacterial pyrazinamidase (pncA) converts it to active pyrazinoic acid (UK §5.1). Active only at slightly acidic pH; bacteriostatic or bactericidal depending on concentration; the exact mechanism is unknown (US Clin Pharm; UK §5.1). Resistance: pncA gene/promoter mutations (70–97% of resistant isolates). Resistance develops rapidly with monotherapy. Cross-resistance only with morphazinamide. Little or no activity against most M. bovis strains and atypical mycobacteria (UK §5.1). PK: CSF ≈ plasma with inflamed meninges; t½ 9–10 h; hepatic hydrolysis to pyrazinoic acid, then renal excretion (~70% in 24 h) (US)

**Why:** The column is empty. The labels describe the mechanism, resistance and PK as given here.

### B9 · Mechanism

Nicotinamide analogue prodrug → converted by mycobacterial pyrazinamidase (pncA) to pyrazinoic acid; the exact target is unknown (US; UK §5.1). Active only at slightly acidic pH (US). Almost no early bactericidal activity, but key to durable, relapse-free cure (sterilizing effect) (PMID 27516382). Bacteriostatic or bactericidal depending on concentration (US). Resistance: pncA/promoter mutations in ~70–97% of resistant isolates; develops rapidly with monotherapy; cross-resistance only with morphazinamide (UK §5.1). M. bovis (incl. BCG) and atypical mycobacteria: hardly or not susceptible (UK §4.1/§5.1; PMID 27516382). PK: Tmax ≤2 h; CSF ≈ plasma with inflamed meninges; ~10% protein bound; t½ 9–10 h; hepatic hydrolysis to pyrazinoic acid; ~70% excreted in urine, mostly as metabolites (US; UK §5.2)

**Why:** Empty column. Every statement is taken from the US Clinical Pharmacology section, UK §5.1/§5.2 and the 2016 guideline's text on PZA's sterilizing effect and pncA, which I verified on the IDSA page.
